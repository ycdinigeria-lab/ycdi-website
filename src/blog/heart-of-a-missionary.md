Every missionary story starts somewhere. This one starts with a conversation on a beach, between a risen Jesus and a disciple who had failed Him. It is one of the most tender exchanges in Scripture, and it is where the call to reach young people really begins.

> So when they had dined, Jesus saith to Simon Peter, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me more than these? He saith unto him, Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee. He saith unto him, Feed my lambs.
>
> John 21:15 (KJV)

Three times Jesus asks the question. Three times Peter answers. And three times the reply is the same: feed my lambs, feed my sheep, feed my sheep. Notice what Jesus does not ask. He does not ask about Peter's gifting, his eloquence, or his track record. He asks about love. The heart of a missionary is not talent. It is love for Christ that spills over into care for people.

That order matters. Mission is not first a strategy or a programme. It is the natural overflow of a heart that loves Jesus. When the love is real, the feeding follows. You start to notice the young person nobody else notices. You give your Saturday. You keep going back to the school that was cold to you the first time. Not because you are paid to, but because you love the One who asked you to.

And the assignment is specific: lambs. The young. The ones still forming, still deciding who they will become. Jesus entrusts the most vulnerable to the ones who love Him most. That is the work in every YCDI classroom and fellowship. It is not crowd management. It is shepherding, one young life at a time.

So the question that started Peter's mission is still the one that starts ours. Not "are you skilled enough" but "do you love Him?" If the answer is yes, the next line is already written. Feed His lambs.
