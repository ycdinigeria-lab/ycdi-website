Trust & safety  /  Safeguarding

Protecting children is not negotiable.

We work with young people every day, so their safety comes before everything else we do.

Our commitment

A child, for us, is anyone under eighteen. We are unconditionally committed to the safety and dignity of every child and young person who comes into contact with YCDI. That commitment is written into our policies and it's a condition of serving with us. No exceptions, at any level.

But whoso shall offend one of these little ones which believe in me, it were better for him that a millstone were hanged about his neck.

Matthew 18:6 (KJV)
How we keep young people safe

Five standards we hold, without exception.

We screen everyone

Nobody works with young people in YCDI without an application, references including a church reference, a safeguarding declaration, and orientation first. We'd rather take our time than deploy someone quickly and cause harm that can't be undone.

Never alone with a child

The two-adult rule applies at all times. No volunteer is alone with a young person in a private or unobserved place.

No private contact

Our volunteers don't message young people privately through personal phones, apps or social media. Contact happens through YCDI's own, open channels.

Clear standards

Every volunteer signs a code of conduct covering physical contact, communication, gifts and language. Any breach is grounds for immediate suspension.

We watch, and we act

Coordinators monitor for warning signs, and any concern about a volunteer's conduct triggers an immediate review.

If you are worried about a child

If a child is in immediate danger, don't wait for us. Contact the police on 112 or 199. To report child trafficking, exploitation or abuse, call NAPTIP free on 0800 800 8000.

To raise a safeguarding concern with YCDI, write to info@ycdinigeria.org marked for the attention of safeguarding, and it will go straight to the people responsible. We treat every report in confidence, we don't ignore or minimise concerns, and where the law requires it we report to the Nigerian authorities.

If you are a young person and something is wrong

If an adult has made you uncomfortable or unsafe, please tell someone you trust. A parent, a teacher, or another trusted adult. You can also ask them to contact YCDI on your behalf using the email above. You were right to speak up, and you will not be in trouble for it.
